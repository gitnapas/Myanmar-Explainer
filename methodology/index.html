Methodology

Mapping an active war invites a specific kind of dishonesty: a confident fill where there should be a question. These are the rules this project works to.

What control means here

Control is not one thing. A force can be present in a town without administering it; it can administer without being accepted; it can be accepted locally while recognised nowhere. This site keeps three ideas apart:

Military presence
Armed forces operate in the area. Says nothing about who runs it.
Administration
Somebody delivers services, resolves disputes and collects revenue.
Legitimacy
Some population or government accepts that authority as rightful.

The closing section of the story asks who governs, who controls and who claims to represent Myanmar as three separate questions precisely because they produce three different maps.

Why the territory layers are coarse

Territorial claims are made at whole state and region resolution, and never below it. That is deliberately coarser than the reporting you may have read, which often describes individual townships or bases.

The reason is that this project has no verified township-level control dataset of its own. Drawing a precise boundary from imprecise reporting produces something that looks like evidence and is not. A state-wide wash that the legend admits is approximate is less impressive and more honest.

Every territorial claim carries an as-of date and a confidence level, and the data model rejects any claim missing either.

Four confidence levels

Documented
Multiple independent sources, or a fact no party disputes.
Probable
Credible reporting, but not independently corroborated.
Contested
Sources actively disagree, or both parties operate throughout.
Unclear
Insufficient evidence. Shown as absence, never as a colour.

Confidence is carried by texture, not by hue. A hatched area means weaker evidence, not a different actor. If uncertainty had its own colour it would look like a finding.

What conflict data does not establish

Conflict event datasets record that something happened at a location. They do not record who held that location afterwards. Inferring control from event density is a common and serious error, and this site does not do it. Event data is used to describe where fighting occurred, and for nothing else.

Proxies, labelled as proxies

Two visual proxies appear in the story, and both are labelled in the legend where they are used:

Protest circles
Sized by city population, not by crowd size. No reliable crowd counts exist for 1988 or 2021.
Movement arcs
An arc runs from one region toward another. It is not a route — journey-level data does not exist.

Naming

Where names are contested, the contest is part of the history. The map carries both the post-1989 names and the older forms still used by many sources and communities — Kayah and Karenni, Kayin and Karen, Rakhine and Arakan. Body text uses the form the cited source uses.

The knowledge boundary

This work in progress was reviewed through 20 September 2026. Fast-moving claims — especially territorial control, casualty totals and the military-run election — use dated sources and explicit uncertainty notes. Where the available evidence does not support a precise map or number, that limitation remains a declared gap. Nine such gaps are listed at the end of the story and reproduced below.

Declared gaps

  • Township-level territorial control for September 2026, with per-township confidence and an as-of date, from IISS Myanmar Conflict Map and/or SAC-M.

    The closing layer is deliberately limited to conservative state-level orientation. Control in an active war changes quickly, and converting a community reference image into precise front lines would imply evidence the project does not have.

  • An independently reconciled constituency-level dataset for the 2025–26 military-run election, including cancellations, uncontested races and the areas where no voting occurred.

    The current step reports official aggregate results and independently documented exclusions. Published accounts disagree on the exact township coverage, so the explainer does not present a single count as settled.

  • A standardised cross-election table for 1990, 2015, 2020 and 2025–26, separating elected seats, appointed military seats, vacancies and contests across different levels of government.

    Published figures differ depending on whether they count elected seats only, both chambers, or the full legislature including the reserved 25 per cent. A comparison across four elections is worthless unless the denominator is consistent.

  • AAPP documented killings and detentions, each quoted with its as-of date.

    AAPP figures rise continuously and are a verified floor, not a total. A number without a date implies a precision and a finality that the source does not claim.

  • Internal displacement by state and refugee populations by destination country, from UN OCHA and UNHCR, with as-of dates.

    The human layer of the final map depends on these. Round figures in general circulation are frequently out of date or conflate IDPs with refugees.

  • Coordinates for Laiza, the KIO/KIA headquarters, from a citable gazetteer.

    Laiza has no GeoNames entry. It is named in the narrative but carries no map pin, because an approximate pin on a headquarters location would look like a sourced fact.

  • The full list of Nationwide Ceasefire Agreement signatories and non-signatories, including organisations that signed after October 2015.

    The point of the signatory network visualisation is precisely who was in and who was out. Getting the membership approximately right would defeat it.

  • A source-backed wind-field or damage-extent layer for Cyclone Nargis, separate from its NOAA best-track line.

    The current animation uses selected NOAA IBTrACS positions and therefore shows direction and landfall only. It must not be read as the width of the storm or the boundary of damage.

  • Which Karenni townships the Interim Executive Council administers, and which functions it delivers in each, from ISP-Myanmar or comparable field research.

    The Karenni case study is meant to show what revolutionary government actually looks like in practice. Without township-level detail it becomes an assertion that it exists.

Sources

  • Michael W. Charney, A History of Modern Burma

    Cambridge University Press · 2009

    The standard single-volume scholarly history, running from the fall of the Konbaung dynasty to 2008. Used here as the general spine for the colonial and post-independence chapters.

  • Angelene Naw, Aung San and the Struggle for Burmese Independence

    Silkworm Books · 2001

    Biography built on British archival records and interviews. The detailed source for Aung San, the Thakin movement and the independence negotiations. Also readable at archive.org/details/aungsanstrugglef0000nawa.

  • Mary P. Callahan, Making Enemies: War and State Building in Burma

    Cornell University Press · 2003

    Argues the army's dominance grew out of war and state formation between 1826 and 1962. Callahan was the first researcher given access to the Burmese military archives, which is why this is the reference for how the Tatmadaw became the state.

  • Thant Myint-U, The Hidden History of Burma

    W. W. Norton · 2019

    Account of the 2011-2020 opening and its collapse, by a historian who worked inside the reform process. Partisan in the sense that the author was a participant, and read here as informed testimony rather than neutral survey.

  • Emergence of the Dobama Asiayone and the Thakins

    Okayama University Scholarly Repository

    Open-access study of how the Dobama Asiayone formed and why its members adopted the title Thakin. Establishes the movement's origins and composition.

  • Nick Cheesman, How in Myanmar 'National Races' Came to Surpass Citizenship and Exclude Rohingya

    Journal of Contemporary Asia 47(3) · 2017

    Shows that taingyintha ('national races') as a political category is a relatively recent invention that the post-1962 state made the basis of citizenship. This is the analytical source for the 1982 law. Paywalled.

  • Panglong Agreement, 12 February 1947

    United Nations Peacemaker · 1947-02-12

    The agreement text itself. Establishes exactly what was signed and by whom -- which is the point, because what Panglong is remembered as promising is considerably more than what it says.

  • Crackdown: Repression of the 2007 Popular Protests in Burma

    Human Rights Watch · 2007-12-06

    Documents the August 2007 fuel price rise, the protests that followed, and the crackdown on them.

  • The Day Three Myanmar Banknotes Suddenly Became Worthless

    The Irrawaddy · 1987-09-05

    Account of the 5 September 1987 demonetisation, which voided the 25, 35 and 75 kyat notes without compensation. The Irrawaddy is an exile-founded outlet, long critical of the military.

  • The repression of the 8-8-88 uprising in Burma/Myanmar

    Sciences Po, Mass Violence and Resistance Research Network

    Scholarly reconstruction of the August 1988 crackdown. Explicit that no agreed death toll exists.

  • Myanmar country overview and economic monitoring

    World Bank

    Source for growth averaging about 6 per cent a year from 2011 to 2019, and for the roughly 18 per cent contraction in the year following the coup.

  • GDP growth (annual %) - Myanmar

    World Bank Open Data

    The underlying annual series. Myanmar's national accounts are weak and post-2021 figures are estimates built on limited reporting, so the shape of the curve is more reliable than any single year.

  • Myanmar Opens Its Mobile Phone Market

    Bloomberg Businessweek · 2014-09-29

    Reports SIM cards falling from over 2,000 US dollars to 1.50 after the 2013 licensing of Telenor and Ooredoo. The single clearest consumer-level measure of what liberalisation changed.

  • Constitution of the Republic of the Union of Myanmar (2008)

    Republic of the Union of Myanmar · 2008

    Primary text. Establishes the reserved military seats, the three security ministries and the amendment threshold.

  • Nationwide Ceasefire Agreement

    Government of Myanmar and signatory ethnic armed organisations · 2015-10-15

    Primary text. Establishes who signed and what was agreed; it does not establish who was excluded or why.

  • Federal Democracy Charter

    Committee Representing Pyidaungsu Hluttaw / National Unity Consultative Council · 2021-03-31

    Primary text of the post-coup opposition's constitutional framework. States intentions and structures, not their implementation.

  • Briefing Paper: Effective Control in Myanmar

    Special Advisory Council for Myanmar · 2022-09

    Assesses territorial control by township with an explicit methodology. Produced by a body sympathetic to the opposition, which is why its figures appear here as one assessment among several rather than as settled fact.

  • Myanmar country analysis

    International Crisis Group

    Ongoing reporting on the armed conflict, the resistance organisations and regional diplomacy.

  • Reports on the human rights situation in Myanmar

    UN Human Rights Office (OHCHR)

    UN human rights reporting, including post-coup documentation.

  • Myanmar Humanitarian Update

    UN Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs

    Displacement and humanitarian access figures. Figures are periodically revised, so any number taken from here must carry its as-of date.

  • Rohingya emergency

    UNHCR

    Registered refugee populations in Bangladesh.

  • Application of the Genocide Convention (The Gambia v. Myanmar)

    International Court of Justice · 2019

    Case record. Establishes the existence and procedural history of the proceedings, not findings on the merits.

  • Daily briefing and post-coup casualty documentation

    Assistance Association for Political Prisoners (Burma)

    The most-cited running tally of post-coup killings and detentions. AAPP counts only cases it can verify, so its figures are explicitly a documented floor rather than an estimate of the total.

  • Myanmar conflict event data

    Armed Conflict Location and Event Data Project

    Records that a conflict event occurred at a location. It does NOT establish who controls that location, and is not used here to infer control.

  • Myanmar Conflict Map

    International Institute for Strategic Studies

    Territorial control assessment with a published methodology and its own stated uncertainty.

  • Myanmar research and analysis

    Institute for Strategy and Policy - Myanmar

    Myanmar-based research on governance, administration and the post-coup political landscape.

  • Myanmar electoral and constitutional analysis

    International IDEA

    Electoral system and constitutional process analysis.

  • Myanmar 1990 election and elected parliamentarians

    Inter-Parliamentary Union · 1999-04-16

    Records the NLD winning 392 of 485 declared seats and the authorities' refusal to transfer power or convene the elected parliament.

  • Announcement 34/2026: Secured votes in the 2025 multiparty general election

    Myanmar Union Election Commission · 2026-02-09

    Official results published through the state-run Global New Light of Myanmar. Used for the commission's vote totals and geographic scope, not as independent validation of the election.

  • Myanmar's military-backed party wins election amid opposition exclusion

    Associated Press · 2026-02-04

    Independent account of the election conditions, opposition exclusion, areas without voting and the continuing constitutional military bloc.

  • Resistance Forces' Control Areas Expanded After Operation 1027

    ISP-Myanmar · 2026-06-05

    Estimates that 39.34 per cent of national landmass was outside Myanmar Armed Forces control. The figure is a dated assessment, not a live territorial ledger.

  • Myanmar civil war simplified.svg

    Wikimedia Commons · 2026-07-11

    CC0 community-maintained cartography used as a dated visual reference for the closing map. Its revision history and source discussions are part of the evidence and the map should not be treated as authoritative.

  • M 7.7 - 2025 Mandalay, Burma (Myanmar) Earthquake

    U.S. Geological Survey · 2025-03-28

    Primary event parameters and impact compilation: epicentre, depth, intensity, confirmed surface rupture and the casualty, injury and missing-person totals used here.

  • Myanmar Economic Monitor: Economic Aftershocks

    World Bank · 2025-06-30

    Estimates 17 million people affected, nine million severely, more than 207,000 displaced, and US$11 billion in direct physical damage, equivalent to 14 per cent of GDP.

  • International Best Track Archive for Climate Stewardship (IBTrACS)

    NOAA National Centers for Environmental Information

    The merged global best-track archive used for the simplified Cyclone Nargis path. The interface deliberately samples the track and does not infer the storm's wind field or damage footprint from it.

  • The 2020 Myanmar General Elections: Democracy Under Attack

    Asian Network for Free Elections · 2021-05

    Final observation report. Records 396 NLD seats and 33 USDP seats among 476 elected Union Parliament seats, alongside cancellations and wider electoral shortcomings.

  • Preliminary Statement on the 2020 Myanmar General Elections

    The Carter Center · 2020-11-10

    Observers assessed election day positively at most polling stations visited and reported no major irregularities, while identifying serious structural and procedural shortcomings.

  • Situation of human rights in Myanmar

    United Nations Human Rights Council · 2024-03-20

    Documents the Three Brotherhood Alliance offensive in northern Shan and distinguishes it from the Arakan Army campaign in Rakhine and Paletwa.

  • Hugh Tinker, The Union of Burma: A Study of the First Years of Independence

    Oxford University Press · 1957

    Near-contemporary survey used for the broad 1948 and 1953 insurgent-activity reconstructions. Its maps are interpretive snapshots, not surveyed front lines.

  • The Current Situation in Burma

    U.S. Department of State, Office of the Historian · 1951-08-01

    Contemporary U.S. intelligence estimate identifying the CPB, KNDO and PVO remnants as principal insurgent forces and noting government recovery of the main population centres.

  • Probable Developments in Burma Through 1953

    U.S. Department of State, Office of the Historian · 1952-08-07

    Contemporary estimate of Communist, Karen and Chinese Nationalist capabilities. Used to check the 1953 actor framing, not to claim exact territorial borders.

  • Jacques P. Leider, Violence and Belonging: Conflict, War, and Insecurity in Arakan, 1942–1952

    ISEAS – Yusof Ishak Institute · 2023

    Historical study of wartime and early post-independence violence in Arakan, including the Mujahid movement from 1948.

    Wikimedia Commons · 2024-03-27

    CC BY-SA 4.0 reconstruction by BorysMapping based on Hugh Tinker's 1957 study. The site redraws its broad activity areas in native SVG and labels their uncertainty.

    Wikimedia Commons · 2024-03-27

    CC BY-SA 4.0 reconstruction by BorysMapping based on Hugh Tinker's 1957 study. The site redraws its broad activity areas in native SVG and labels their uncertainty.

  • Historical Notes on Burma / Myanmar

    Network Myanmar · 2022

    Used for U Nu’s absence, the attempt at his home, and the reconstitution of the Executive Council after the assassination.

  • The Death of Aung San in 1947 — An Important Clarification

    Network Myanmar · 2022

    Detailed review of the assassination record and Galon U Saw’s conviction.

  • Telegram from Rangoon, 19 July 1947

    U.S. Department of State, Office of the Historian · 1947-07-19

    Contemporary diplomatic report identifying U Ohn Maung as Deputy Secretary of Transport and Communications and recording his death.

  • Burma/Myanmar political chronology and historical studies

    Institute of Developing Economies, Japan External Trade Organization

    Used for the post-assassination political and military leadership context.

  • Burma to Myanmar: large print exhibition guide

    The British Museum · 2023

    Summarises Britain’s three-stage annexation in 1824–26, 1852 and 1885.
